T1 afternoon pressure was higher than morning pressure on every June day

Recorded
In all 30 June days checked, no position’s afternoon mean (14:00 to 16:00) was below its morning mean (05:00 to 07:00). Fleet mean pressure change ranged from +2.0 to +13.8 PSI and temperature change from +2.2 to +19.1 °C.
Reading limit. A rise is expected as tyres warm. These window means neither show nor rule out a slow leak, and they can miss short events at the alert times.

Running pressures sit well above cold targets

Recorded
Drive tyres with a 100 PSI cold target were recorded at 132 to 134 PSI while running. Operating pressure rose 15 to 30 PSI above cold baselines.
Reading limit. Running readings are not cold-pressure checks and should not be compared with the cold specification.

Scope and sources

The analysis uses only the files supplied by the client. No values were estimated, projected or filled in.

Telemetry

12 TyreSense CSV exports covering June and July 2026: pressure, temperature, sensor battery and GPS, read from valve-stem sensors.

Registers and logs

Vehicle register, daily odometer logs (clerk or driver reported) and the client’s data dictionary.

Inspections

Tread-depth (NSD) inspection sheets, including tyre serial numbers, for T1 to T4.

How to read a position code

The first digit is the axle (1 to 5). L or R is the side. O or I is outer or inner on dual (twin) axles. For example, 5RI is axle 5, right side, inner tyre. Axles 1 to 3 carry single tyres; axles 4 and 5 carry dual tyres. Pressures are in PSI and temperatures in °C.

Fleet and depot context

The two depots follow different pressure-check routines, which shapes how their readings can be used.

Belagavi depot

Trucks T1 (Goa), T2 (Karwar) and T6 (Vijayapura). Long-distance runs. Crews check pressure first thing in the morning, before departure.

Check (tyres cold)›Depart›Run

Hubballi depot

Trucks T3 (Gadag), T4 (City) and T5 (Shivamogga). Regional and city runs. Crews check and inflate in the afternoon, after trucks return.

Run›Return›Check and inflate (tyres warm)›Cool overnight›Depart

Data preparation

Three data issues were resolved before any figure in this report was calculated.

June and July exports overlap by 12 hours

June files run to about 06:00 on 1 July; July files start at about 18:00 on 30 June.
Combining the files gave 559,179 rows. Exact duplicate records in the overlap (2,869 rows) were removed before final calculations, leaving 556,310 rows (559,179 − 2,869).

Registration typo

27,082 rows carry KA25B4471 instead of KA25AB4471 (T1). The registration was standardised so the truck’s rows are not split.

Side codes in the data dictionary contradict each other

The dictionary defines C as the co-driver side (right) and D as the driver side (left), yet in the same paragraph lists CF1 as front left. Matching tyre serial numbers in the tread-inspection sheet indicates C is the left (co-driver) side and D is the right (driver) side.

Findings in this report are anchored to TyreSense position codes such as 5RI, not to workshop side codes. The serial-to-position link should be confirmed against the maintenance log.

Heat and pressure

Tyre air pressure rises with temperature (Gay-Lussac’s law: at constant volume, pressure is proportional to absolute temperature). Operating readings therefore sit above cold targets.

Cold target against recorded pressures

Drive tyres and Hubballi morning departure readings.
Running tyres reached 67 to 73 °C on drive axles and 50 to 52 °C on steer axles, against ambient of about 20 to 22 °C. T1’s run includes a 22 km stretch of the Anmod Ghat.

T1 June comparison: afternoon against morning

Goa truck, all 30 June days.
Method: for each of 14 active positions, mean sensor readings at 05:00 to 07:00 were compared with 14:00 to 16:00. Every position had at least 10 readings per window. No temperature correction was applied.

What this comparison shows

  • Afternoon mean pressure was higher than morning on all 30 days, for all 14 positions.
  • Mean temperature rose on the same days, which is consistent with warming.
  • Running pressures should be compared with cold targets only after a cold check.

What it does not show

  • Whether a slow leak is present. Daily temperature swings can hide one unless readings are corrected to a common temperature.
  • Why the managing director saw 14 low-pressure alerts every afternoon. Alert rules and timestamps are not in the export.
  • Whether any alert was false, or caused by rain. These labels should not be applied.

Lowest sensor battery reading by truck

Percent. T2 shows the range across 3RO, 2LO and 5LO.

Reading the battery results

  • T5 position 2RO fell from 14% to 1% by mid-July, the lowest reading in the fleet.
  • T3 5RO (about 43%) and T4 4RI (about 50%) are the next lowest single readings.
  • Battery percentage is an automated sensor indicator. A sensor that is still transmitting can report a low percentage.
  • Test sensor voltage during tyre servicing before any sensor is replaced.

What the data can establish

The boundary between supported statements and statements that need more evidence.

Supported by the pack

  • T2 position 5RI recorded 102.9, 48.9 and 13.9 PSI at 00:00, 11:10 and 14:17 on 11 July, then no further readings from that sensor.
  • T1 afternoon mean pressure was above morning mean on all 30 June days, alongside higher temperatures.
  • Running pressures are well above cold targets.
  • The June and July exports overlap by 12 hours, with 2,869 duplicate rows.
  • T5 and T6 have no tread-depth records.

Not established by the pack

  • Whether any alert fired, reached the driver or was acknowledged.
  • The physical cause of any pressure difference or of the T2 event.
  • Whether a depot’s gauge or a TyreSense sensor is the more accurate.
  • Any effect of load, route or road condition on pressure or wear.
  • That a one-day-ahead failure warning would work. One recorded failure event cannot validate a predictive model.

Next steps

Records and checks that would close the gaps listed above.

Alert records

Export alert creation, delivery and acknowledgement timestamps and the alert rules from TyreSense.

Calibrated cold checks

Compare depot dial gauges with a certified reference and with TyreSense. Run cold-pressure checks on the same routine at both depots.

Operating context

Record load weights and trip logs for each run.

Serial and position mapping

Confirm the C/D side-code mapping and tyre serial positions against the maintenance log at the next service.

Tread coverage

Inspect T5 and T6, and align inspection dates across the fleet.

Sensor and odometer checks

Test T5 2RO sensor voltage before replacement. Verify repetitive odometer entries against source logs.

Testing a one-day-ahead warningA prospective test would need exact alert delivery times, physical maintenance records, calibrated cold-pressure checks, and several confirmed failure events.
Central limitation. This pack can identify patterns and investigation priorities, but it cannot establish every physical cause or validate one-day-ahead failure prediction.
